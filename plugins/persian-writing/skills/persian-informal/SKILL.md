# Informal Persian writing

Warm, direct, spoken-sounding — but still correctly spelled. Casual is a register, not a licence for sloppy orthography.

## Tone

- Write it the way you'd say it: short sentences, everyday words.
- Verb placement is free here — spoken Persian happily puts things after the verb («رفتم خونه‌شون، دیشب»).
- Pick تو or شما and hold it for the whole message.
- Drop bureaucratic vocabulary completely: احتراماً، خواهشمند است، مبذول، فوق‌الذکر، مقتضی، عطف به.
- Ask directly — «می‌تونی تا فردا نگاهش کنی؟» — not through formulas.
- Emoji: one or two at most, and only if the reader's own style uses them.

## Colloquial spelling (شکسته‌نویسی)

The test is syllable reduction — break a word only when the spoken form is shorter than the written one.

- Break: می‌روم → می‌رم، می‌خواهم → می‌خوام، می‌گویم → می‌گم، هندوانه → هندونه، از من → ازم، به او → بهش، با او → باهاش، برای او → براش.
- Don't break: میدان (same syllable count), past forms (رفته‌ام، رفته بودم), polite plurals (میل کنید).
- Everyday usage also breaks خانه → خونه، آن → اون، می‌دانم → می‌دونم without any syllable saving. That's fine in a chat message; keep it out of anything semi-official.
- را → رو is fine in real conversation, but write را in a message to a client or a manager.
- **Consistency is the whole game.** Pick one level of breaking and hold it — mixing می‌روم and می‌رم in one message is what makes casual Persian look wrong.

## Still applies

- نیم‌فاصله (ZWNJ) in the broken forms too: می‌رم، نمی‌دونم، بچه‌ها، بزرگ‌تر.
- Persian letters ی and ک, never Arabic ي and ك.
- ه vs kasra: write ه only where است is meant («دوستمه» = دوستم است). Otherwise it's the ezāfe — «دوستِ من», never «دوسته من».
- Persian punctuation: ، ؟ « ». No space before, one space after.
- Parentheses for asides. A dash reads fine here where it's natural, just don't stack them.
- Right-align every column of any markdown table holding Persian content (`---:` in the separator row).
- Persian or Latin digits are both fine — be consistent within one message.
- English words are fine where they're what people actually say (ددلاین، فایل، ایمیل); wrap code, filenames, and identifiers in `backticks`.

## Length and shape

- A message is a few lines, not a document. No headings; bullets only for a real list.
- If it's growing past a screen, it probably wants the formal register instead.

## Switch registers

Contracts, official letters, reports to clients, and anything legal or accounting-facing are not this skill's job — write those in formal Persian.
